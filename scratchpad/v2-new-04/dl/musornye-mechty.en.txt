'''''Garbage Dreams''''' is a 2009 [[feature length]] [[documentary film]] produced and directed by Mai Iskander.  Filmed over the course of four years, ''Garbage Dreams'' follows three teenage boys growing up in Egypt's garbage village. ''Garbage Dreams'' aired on the [[PBS]] program ''[[Independent Lens]]'' for the occasion of [[Earth Day]] 2010 and has been screened in many international film festivals.

==Synopsis==
{{more plot|date=November 2015}}
''Garbage Dreams'' follows three teenage boys born into the trash trade and growing up in the world's largest garbage village, on the outskirts of [[Cairo]], [[Egypt]]. It is the home to 60,000 Zaballeen, also spelled "[[Zabbaleen]]" as Arabic for "garbage people." Far ahead of any modern "Green" initiatives, the Zaballeen survive by [[recycling]] 80 percent of the garbage they collect. When their community is suddenly faced with the globalization of its trade, each of the teenage boys is forced to make choices that will impact his future and the survival of his community.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.spotlightradio.net/listen/waste-management-in-cairo |archive-url=https://archive.today/20120910112554/http://www.spotlightradio.net/listen/waste-management-in-cairo |url-status=usurped |archive-date=September 10, 2012 |title= Waste Management in Cairo Website: Zabbaleen community of Cairo}}</ref>

==Release==
''Garbage Dreams'' premiered at the 2009 [[SXSW Film Festival]], where it ran in the US Documentary Competition<ref>{{cite web |url=http://sxsw.com/film/screenings/schedule/?a=show&s=F15102 |title= Garbage Dreams: 2009 SXSW Film Festival}}</ref> ''Garbage Dreams'' had its international premiere in Europe at the twenty-second International Documentary Film Festival Amsterdam<ref>{{cite web |url= http://www.idfa.nl/industry/tags/project.aspx?id=ac1243d6-29da-4875-bb31-9a3320419386 |title= Garbage Dreams |work= idfa.nl |access-date= January 10, 2011 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20110927180107/http://www.idfa.nl/industry/tags/project.aspx?id=ac1243d6-29da-4875-bb31-9a3320419386 |archive-date= September 27, 2011 |url-status= dead |df= mdy-all }}</ref>
and its Middle Eastern premiere at the [[Dubai International Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.dubaifilmfest.com/en/films-explorer/?id=4299 |title=Dubai International Film Festival |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100102031356/http://www.dubaifilmfest.com/en/films-explorer/?id=4299 |archive-date=2010-01-02 }}</ref>

Garbage Dreams was shortlisted for the 2010 Academy Awards in the category of Best Feature Length Documentary, was nominated for the 2010 Best Documentary by the Directors Guild of America, and has won 26 international awards including the Al Gore Reel Current Award <ref>{{cite web |url= http://www.onearth.org/node/1109 |title= On Earth magazine, Al Gore presents REEL Current Award to ''Garbage Dreams''}}</ref> and IDA Humanitas Award.<ref>{{cite web |title= 'Garbage Dreams' Wins the IDS/Humanitas Award |url= http://workingfilms.org/blog/?tag=humanitas-award |publisher= Working Films |date=17 December 2009 |accessdate= 7 November 2012}}</ref>

''Garbage Dreams'' aired on PBS ''Independent Lens'' for the occasion of Earth Day 2010.<ref>{{cite web |title= Garbage Dreams - Independent Lens |url= https://www.pbs.org/independentlens/garbage-dreams/game.html |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20100425132858/http://www.pbs.org/independentlens/garbage-dreams/game.html |url-status= dead |archive-date= April 25, 2010 |publisher= PBS |date=20 April 2010 |accessdate=7 November 2012}}</ref> It has been screened in over 100 international film festivals, including the [[Woodstock Film Festival]],<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.woodstockfilmfestival.com/festival2009/details.php?id=20005 |title= Woodstock Film Festival 2009}}</ref> the [[Hollywood Film Festival]], the [[Bel Air Film Festival]],<ref>{{cite web |url= http://www.ibtimes.com/prnews/20091028/2nd-annual-bel-air-film-festival-announces-line-up-and-honorees.htm |title= International Business Times |website= [[International Business Times]] |url-status= dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20091111150746/http://www.ibtimes.com/prnews/20091028/2nd-annual-bel-air-film-festival-announces-line-up-and-honorees.htm |archive-date= 2009-11-11 }}</ref> [[Maryland Film Festival]], and the [[Seattle International Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.siff.net/cinema/detail.aspx?id=28930&FID=180 |title= SIFF Cinema |url-status=dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20120329132849/http://www.siff.net/cinema/detail.aspx?id=28930&FID=180 |archive-date= 2012-03-29 }}</ref>

==Critical reception==
Former U.S. [[Vice President of the United States|Vice President]] [[Al Gore]] presented the 2009 REEL Current Award to the documentary ''Garbage Dreams'' at the [[Nashville Film Festival]]. Gore, and the writer of ''[[An Inconvenient Truth]]'', presents the award annually to a film that gives outstanding insight into a contemporary global issue.

Gore said of the film, "''Garbage Dreams'' is a moving story of young men searching for a ways to eke out a living for their families and facing tough choices as they try to do the right thing for the planet. Mai Iskander guides us into a 'garbage village', a place so different from our own, and yet the choices they face there are so hauntingly familiar. Ultimately, ''Garbage Dreams'' makes a compelling case that modernization does not always equal progress."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.cinemaspy.ca/article.php?id=2337 |title=Cinemaspy, ''Garbage Dreams is the Reel Thing'' }}{{dead link|date=January 2017 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref>

In ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' Ronnie Scheib called the film "Stunning debut ...  [Iskander's] lensing grants her subjects immense dignity (they never appear "other" in their poverty) and her film its curious beauty."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.variety.com/review/VE1117941838.html?categoryid=1237&cs=1|title=Variety,''Garbage Dreams'' | first=Ronnie | last=Scheib | date=2010-01-04}}</ref>

In her review in ''[[The New York Times]]'', Jeannette Catsoulis said "Expertly weaving personal fears, family tensions and political action, 'Garbage Dreams' records the tremblings of a culture at a crossroads."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://movies.nytimes.com/2010/01/06/movies/06garbage.html?hpw|title=The New York Times, ''The Struggles of Cairo's 'Garbage People,' but No Whining'' | first=Jeannette | last=Catsoulis |work=The New York Times | accessdate=2010-05-03 | date=2010-01-06}}</ref>

In ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' [[Frank Scheck]] wrote "Championed by Oscar winner Al Gore and the spur for a million-dollar donation by the [[Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation]], ''Garbage Dreams '' could ride its sociological importance to Oscar recognition."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/hr/film-reviews/garbage-dreams-film-review-1004057492.story|title=The Hollywood Reporter, ''Garbage Dreams -- Film Review'' | first=Frank | last=Scheck|date=October 14, 2010}}</ref>

In ''[[The Village Voice]]'' Andrew Schenker called ''Garbage Dreams'' a "handsomely shot and intermittently fascinating look at Cairo's Zaballeen community."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.villagevoice.com/2010-01-05/film/cairo-s-trash-collectors-face-extinction-in-garbage-dreams|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20100114051811/http://www.villagevoice.com/2010-01-05/film/cairo-s-trash-collectors-face-extinction-in-garbage-dreams/|url-status=dead|archive-date=January 14, 2010|title=The Village Voice, ''Cairo's Trash Collectors Face Extinction in Garbage Dreams''}}</ref>
