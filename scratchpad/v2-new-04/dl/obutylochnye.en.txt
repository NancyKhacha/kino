'''''Tapped''''' is a 2009 documentary film by directors [[Stephanie Soechtig]] and Jason Lindsey.<ref>[https://www.cbsnews.com/news/katiecouric-taps-into-the-bottled-water-debate-with-joe-doss-and-stephanie-soechtig/ @katiecouric Taps Into the Bottled Water Debate with Joe Doss and Stephanie Soechtig] CBS News</ref> The two began the documentary after research into [[Marine pollution|ocean pollution]] "kept leading them to bottled water".<ref>[http://www.greenwichtime.com/default/article/Greenwich-s-Nestle-targeted-by-water-documentary-260661.php Greenwich's Nestle targeted by water documentary] Greenwich Time</ref>

==Synopsis==
''Tapped'' looks into the [[bottled water]] industry and its long-term effects socially, [[economics|economically]] and [[Ecology|ecologically]].<ref>[http://www.coloradodaily.com/ci_14788513#axzz1xt7No5KR 'Tapped' documentary at CU-Boulder raises recycling awareness] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140929002551/http://www.coloradodaily.com/ci_14788513#axzz1xt7No5KR |date=2014-09-29 }} Colorado Daily</ref> The filmmakers focused on industry giants such as [[PepsiCo]] and [[Nestlé Waters]], visiting a town containing a Nestlé factory as well as running tests on the bottles the company uses for its products. Their results came back showing "several potentially harmful chemicals, some known carcinogens".<ref name="CTPOST">[http://www.ctpost.com/news/article/Nestle-Waters-CEO-defends-company-at-documentary-461439.php Nestle Waters CEO defends company at documentary screening] CT Post</ref> The documentary also focused on the number of bottles that are recycled, noting that "Forty percent of bottled water is really just filtered [[tap water]], and every day we throw away 30 million single-served bottles of water as [[plastic waste]]."<ref>[https://www.cbsnews.com/news/filmmakers-investigate-bottled-water-industry/ Filmmakers Investigate Bottled Water Industry] CBS News</ref>

==Reception==
''[[Grist (magazine)|Grist]]'' wrote "A few too many mid-interview cutaways to Soechtig looking concerned came off as a little journalistically self-important, but ''Tapped'' does a solid job of covering every aspect of this damaging industry and inspiring more outrage than despair."<ref>[https://grist.org/article/2009-08-03-tapped-documentary-pulls-plug-on-bottled-water-craze/ ‘Tapped’ documentary pulls plug on bottled water craze] Grist</ref> The ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' praised the film, stating that the film was "persuasive" and a "compact, clear-headed documentary".<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20101226034852/http://articles.latimes.com/2009/aug/07/entertainment/et-capsules7/2 'El Tinte de la Fama' Also: 'Cold Souls,' 'Somers Town,' 'Tapped' and '12 in a Box'] LA Times</ref> [[NPR]]'s Glenn McDonald praised the film but criticized some of the editing done to "ridicule industry spokespeople", stating that it was unnecessary as the film's content "sells itself".<ref>[https://www.npr.org/blogs/monkeysee/2010/08/10/129103673/-tapped-just-another-excellent-indie-documentary-likely-to-bring-you-down 'Tapped': Just Another Excellent Indie Documentary Likely To Bring You Down] NPR</ref>

===Response by bottling companies===
President and CEO of Nestlé Waters North America (until 2013<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.greenbiz.com/blog/2013/03/04/exit-interview-kim-jeffrey-nestle-water|title=Exit Interview: Kim Jeffery, Nestle Water|work=GreenBiz}}</ref>) [[Kim Jeffery]]  responded to several of the questions brought up by the film, stating that the bottles used for the products were safe and that one of the chemicals discovered in the tests, [[bisphenol A]] was "in the liners of all [[canning|canned foods]] to prevent [[botulism]], and in the DVDs of the documentary that people were able to purchase".<ref name="CTPOST" />
