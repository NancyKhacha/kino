'''''Lilo & Stitch''''' is a 2025 American [[science fiction film|science fiction]] [[comedy-drama film]] produced by [[Walt Disney Pictures]] and [[Rideback (production company)|Rideback]]. Directed by [[Dean Fleischer Camp]], it is a [[live-action animated film|live-action animated]] remake of [[Walt Disney Animation Studios|Disney's]] 2002 traditionally animated film ''[[Lilo & Stitch]]''. The film stars [[Sydney Elizebeth Agudong]], [[Billy Magnussen]], [[Hannah Waddingham]], [[Courtney B. Vance]], [[Zach Galifianakis]], and [[Maia Kealoha]] in her film debut, with original ''Lilo & Stitch'' writer-director [[Chris Sanders]] reprising his voice role as [[Stitch (Lilo & Stitch)|Stitch]].

Development on a live-action remake of ''Lilo & Stitch'' began in October 2018, with writer Mike Van Waes and producers [[Dan Lin]] and Jonathan Eirich attached. By November 2020, [[Jon M. Chu]] was in talks to direct, while Van Waes left the project despite still being credited. Fleischer Camp and Chris Kekaniokalani Bright were announced as the film's new director and writer respectively in July 2022, with casting taking place between November 2022 and June 2023. [[Principal photography]] ran from April to July 2023, filming during the [[2023 Writers Guild of America strike]] until suspending production due to the [[2023 SAG-AFTRA strike]], and then in February and March 2024 after [[2023 Hollywood labor disputes|both strikes]] ended. [[Dan Romer]] composed the score, and [[Industrial Light & Magic]] provided the film's visual effects with the assistance of several other effects houses.

''Lilo & Stitch'' premiered at the [[El Capitan Theatre]] in [[Los Angeles]] on May 17, 2025, and was released in the United States on May 23, by [[Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures]]. The film received generally positive reviews from critics, and grossed $1.038{{nbsp}}billion worldwide, becoming the [[2025 in film#Highest-grossing films|fourth-highest-grossing film of 2025]] and the [[List of highest-grossing live-action/animated films|highest-grossing live-action/animated hybrid in history]], being the first of its kind to gross over $1{{nbsp}}billion. A sequel, with Sanders writing and directing, is in development for release on May 26, 2028.

==Plot==
<!--Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. Please check the word count before making any additions. Word Count as of September 2, 2025: 677-->
On planet Turo, [[Dr. Jumba Jookiba]] is convicted by the United Galactic Federation of illegal genetic experimentation, for creating an aggressive, near-indestructible creature with advanced learning capabilities called [[Stitch (Lilo & Stitch)|Experiment 626]]. Jumba is imprisoned, while 626 is exiled for his destructive behavior. However, 626 escapes by stealing a police cruiser and using its hyperdrive to reach [[Earth]], crashing on [[Kaua{{okina}}i]]. The [[Grand Councilwoman]] offers Jumba freedom if he recaptures 626, pairing him with Earth expert, Agent Pleakley.

A Hawaiian girl named [[Lilo Pelekai]] is expelled from hula school for pushing her bully Mertle off stage due to frustration over her sister [[Nani Pelekai|Nani]]'s absence. Their [[social work]]er, Mrs. Kekoa, visits and finds Nani unfit to care for Lilo, demanding she complete her tasks in three days.<!--The film starts on a Tuesday according to the opening scene, and Mrs. Kekoa, who visits the Pelekais that day, tells Nani to complete her tasks by Friday, three days from then.--> Elsewhere, 626 crashes near a wedding reception and is run over by a tourist trolley before being taken to an [[animal shelter]]. 

The next day, having heard that Lilo wants a friend, their neighbor Tūtū takes Lilo to the animal shelter where 626 is kept. Realizing Jumba and Pleakley are after him, 626 impersonates as a dog and allows Lilo to adopt him for his protection. Nani takes them to her resort lūʻau job with her friend [[David Kawena]]. Lilo names 626 "Stitch" after Nani comments on having to stitch a car seat he tore. They enjoy the resort, but Stitch accidentally causes a table fire, leading to Nani's dismissal.

Nani is visited by Kekoa and Cobra Bubbles, a [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] agent posing as Kekoa's social director to investigate Stitch's arrival. Kekoa insists that Nani find new employment immediately. However, she is rejected after many interviews due to Lilo and Stitch's antics. Lilo helps Nani secure a job as a surfing instructor, and they enjoy surfing after her first shift. Meanwhile, Jumba and Pleakley fail to capture Stitch while jet skiing, causing the Pelekais and Stitch to wipe out, almost drowning Lilo when Stitch sinks.

After Lilo's recovery, Kekoa informs Nani that the [[Government of Hawaii|Hawaiian government]] will cover her health insurance costs if she relinquishes her guardianship—a decision she reluctantly accepts. As the sisters share a final night together, Stitch reflects on his actions and forlornly returns to the animal shelter alone. Meanwhile, the Grand Councilwoman, frustrated with Jumba's failure to capture Stitch, cancels their deal and orders Pleakley to return Jumba to prison. However, Jumba escapes Pleakley and plans to capture Stitch instead.

Kekoa and Bubbles arrive the next morning to find that Lilo is missing, so they and Nani start searching for her. Lilo finds Stitch at the shelter, but Jumba arrives to recapture Stitch. They escape to the Pelekais' home and fight Jumba, destroying their house. During the battle, Jumba reveals that Stitch used Lilo for his protection, leading to a guilt-ridden Stitch's surrender. Jumba takes Stitch aboard his spaceship using a portal-generating gun, intending to erase Stitch's newfound empathy. However, Lilo sneaks aboard and frees Stitch, and they eject Jumba from the ship. The ship crashes into the ocean, trapping Lilo and Stitch underwater. Stitch rescues her, but as he cannot swim to the surface, he releases her to let her emerge and drowns.

Nani and David rescue Lilo, but she refuses to leave Stitch. David returns Lilo to shore while Nani swims back to get Stitch from the ocean floor. They reach shore, but Stitch remains unconscious. After Bubbles finds Pleakley and they rejoin the group, David revives Stitch by [[Jump start (vehicle)|jump-starting]] his lungs. Shortly after, the Grand Councilwoman arrives and re-arrests Jumba. After seeing Stitch's change of heart, she decides to let Stitch exile on Earth with his newfound ''[[ohana|ʻohana]]''. The Grand Councilwoman vows to keep in touch, with Pleakley living with the Pelekais to watch over Stitch. As the Pelekais, Stitch, and company return home, Kekoa tells Nani she can [[hānai|transfer]] Lilo's guardianship to David, Tūtū and Bubbles, letting her stay home. The ''ʻohana'' then repairs the house and lives happily.

In a [[mid-credits scene]], Nani, now attending the [[University of California, San Diego]] to study marine biology with Lilo's blessing, uses Jumba's [[portal gun]] to visit Lilo and Stitch in Kauaʻi.

===Development===
On October 3, 2018, it was announced that [[Walt Disney Pictures]] was developing a live-action/computer-generated animation hybrid film remake of Disney's 2002 animated feature film ''[[Lilo & Stitch]]''. ===Filming===
According to Fleischer Camp, the production team was "so dedicated" to shooting the entire film [[Location shooting|on location]] in Hawaii because "there's just not really a substitute for it".<ref name="Chen">{{cite news |last1=Chen |first1=Kat |title=How the New Lilo & Stitch Reimagines the Original's Hawaiian Backdrop |url=https://www.cntraveler.com/story/lilo-and-stitch-hawaii |access-date=June 10, 2025 |work=Conde Nast Traveler |date=May 23, 2025 |archive-date=May 23, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250523152221/https://www.cntraveler.com/story/lilo-and-stitch-hawaii |url-status=live}}</ref> He vaguely alluded to "boring logistical reasons" for why the film could not be shot on location on the island of Kauai; instead, it was filmed on the island of [[Oahu]].<ref name="Chen" />

===Visual effects and design===
The visual effects were provided by Disney-owned [[Industrial Light & Magic]] (ILM).<ref name="Johnson">{{cite news |last1=Johnson |first1=G. Allen |title=How this S.F. company's $100 million gamble made 'Lilo & Stitch' one of Disney's biggest hits |url=https://www.sfchronicle.com/entertainment/article/lilo-stitch-disney-industrial-light-20322039.php |access-date=June 6, 2025 |work=San Francisco Chronicle |date=June 6, 2025 |archive-date=June 7, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250607031245/https://www.sfchronicle.com/entertainment/article/lilo-stitch-disney-industrial-light-20322039.php |url-status=live}}</ref> [[Craig Hammack]] served as visual effects supervisor; according to Hammack, he first became involved in "serious discussions about the project in the fall of 2022".<ref name="Frei">{{cite news|last1=Frei |first1=Vincent| title= Lilo & Stitch: Craig Hammack (Production VFX Supervisor) & Eric Guaglione (Production Animation Director) |url=https://www.artofvfx.com/lilo-and-stitch-craig-hammack-production-vfx-supervisor-and-eric-guaglione-production-animation-director/ |access-date=June 12, 2025 |work= Art of VFX |date=June 12, 2025 |archive-date=June 15, 2025 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250615085410/https://www.artofvfx.com/lilo-and-stitch-craig-hammack-production-vfx-supervisor-and-eric-guaglione-production-animation-director/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Additional visual effects were provided by [[Luma Pictures]], [[Moving Picture Company]] (MPC), [[Sony Pictures Imageworks]], Untold Studios, [[Crafty Apes]], Atomic Arts and Cantina Creative.<ref name="Frei" />

Early on, to ensure strong on-screen chemistry between the child actress playing Lilo (who had not yet been cast) and the computer-generated Stitch character, ILM decided to employ animatronic puppets as stand-ins for Stitch on set.<ref name="Johnson" /> ILM hired [[Legacy Effects]] to build several puppet versions of Stitch, including a version suitable for underwater scenes.<ref name="Johnson" /> The puppets never appear on screen in the finished film; they were created only to provide the cast members with a "physical scene partner" capable of eliciting appropriate emotional reactions.<ref name="Johnson" /> This method resulted in a lot of "messy plate cleanup" for the visual effects artists (in terms of carefully erasing the puppets from the raw footage).<ref name="Frei" />  However, it was deemed to be worth the cost because of the risk of getting "stale" performances otherwise if the cast had been asked to react to an entirely imaginary Stitch.<ref name="Johnson" /> Legacy Effects sent puppeteer Seth Hayes to Hawaii to operate the puppets on set.<ref name="Johnson" /> For some scenes in which Lilo initially mistakes Stitch for a dog, a [[French Bulldog]] named Dale played Stitch.<ref name="Johnson" /> In post-production, the puppet and dog stand-ins for Stitch were replaced by ILM with a CGI Stitch.<ref name="Johnson" />

There are 1,673 visual effects shots in the final cut of the film.<ref name="Frei" /> Originally, ILM was planning to do the majority of the visual effects work itself, but then had to bring in additional vendors as the complexity of the project became clear.<ref name="Frei" /> One reason for the large number of vendors is that MPC suddenly went into [[Administration in United Kingdom law|administration]] just six weeks before the deadline for delivering their work, and ILM had to scramble to find other companies who could finish MPC's shots on short notice.<ref name="Frei" />

The animators working on the 3D version of Stitch tried to capture the "essence" of the original 2D animated version and "make him fit convincingly" into the real world.<ref name="Frei" /> The "biggest discrepancy" never explained in the film is that Stitch is supposed to be very heavy, yet Lilo is able to pick him up.<ref name="Frei" /> The animators were trying to avoid a [[shot-for-shot]] remake, but did closely follow the look of certain shots in the original film for several iconic moments.<ref name="Frei" />

On July 17, 2025, ''Lilo & Stitch'' crossed the $1 billion threshold, becoming the first Hollywood film of 2025 and second overall film released that year (after the Chinese animated film ''[[Ne Zha 2]]'') to do so. It became the first [[List of remakes and adaptations of Disney animated films|adaptation of a Disney animated film]] to surpass that threshold since ''[[The Lion King (2019 film)|The Lion King]]'' in 2019, and surpassed ''[[The Smurfs (film)|The Smurfs]]'' ($563 million in 2011) to become the [[List of highest-grossing live-action/animated films|highest-grossing]] [[live-action animation|live-action/animated]] film of all time.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/lilo-and-stitch-becomes-first-billion-box-office-1236318553/|title=Box Office Milestone: 'Lilo & Stitch' Becomes First Title of 2025 to Cross $1 Billion Globally|first=Pamela|last=McClintock|publisher=The Hollywood Reporter|date=July 17, 2025|accessdate=July 17, 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://deadline.com/2025/07/lilo-stitch-1-billion-global-box-office-milestone-1236460970/|title='Lilo & Stitch' Crosses $1B Global Box Office, First Hollywood Title Of 2025 To Milestone|first=Nancy|last=Tartaglione|publisher=Deadline Hollywood|date=July 17, 2025|accessdate=July 17, 2025}}</ref>

===Critical response===
The film received mixed-to-positive reviews from critics.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Malhotra |first=Rahul |date=May 25, 2025 |title=''Mission: Impossible – The Final Reckoning'' and ''Lilo & Stitch'' Deliver Record-Breaking Memorial Day Box Office Numbers |url=https://collider.com/mission-impossible-final-reckoning-memorial-day-weekend-domestic-box-office-63-million/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250526004356/https://collider.com/mission-impossible-final-reckoning-memorial-day-weekend-domestic-box-office-63-million/ |archive-date=May 26, 2025 |access-date=May 26, 2025 |website=[[Collider (website)|Collider]] |quote=''Lilo & Stitch'' opened to mixed reviews}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Murphy |first=J. Kim |date=May 24, 2025 |title=Box Office: ''Lilo & Stitch'' Riding the Wave to Biggest Memorial Day Weekend Ever, ''Mission: Impossible'' Cruises to $24.8 Million Opening Day |url=https://variety.com/2025/film/news/box-office-mission-impossible-lilo-stitch-breaking-record-1236408758/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250525001052/https://variety.com/2025/film/news/box-office-mission-impossible-lilo-stitch-breaking-record-1236408758/ |archive-date=May 25, 2025 |access-date=May 26, 2025 |magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |quote=Reviews lean favorable, but audiences seem enthused}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Parkel |first=Inga |date=May 24, 2025 |title=''Lilo & Stitch'' to set Memorial Day box office record despite middling critical reviews |url=https://www.the-independent.com/arts-entertainment/films/news/lilo-and-stitch-box-office-reviews-b2757277.html |url-access=limited |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250526004507/https://www.the-independent.com/arts-entertainment/films/news/lilo-and-stitch-box-office-reviews-b2757277.html |archive-date=May 26, 2025 |access-date=May 26, 2025 |newspaper=[[The Independent]] |quote=The movie's box office success comes in spite of negative reviews from critics}}</ref> {{Rotten Tomatoes prose |score=72|2=6.2|count=218|consensus=Recapturing the adorable charm of the original if not quite matching its rambunctious sense of imagination, ''Lilo & Stitch'' emerges out of the crate as one of the better live-action remakes of a Disney classic. |ref=yes |access-date=June 6, 2026}} [[Metacritic]], which uses a [[Weighted arithmetic mean|weighted average]], assigned the film a score of 53 out of 100, based on 40 critics, indicating "mixed or average" reviews.<ref name="Metacritic">{{cite web |title=''Lilo &#38; Stitch'' |url=https://www.metacritic.com/movie/lilo-stitch-2025 |website=[[Metacritic]] |publisher=[[Fandom (website)#Fandom, Inc.|Fandom, Inc.]] |access-date=June 6, 2026 |archive-url= |archive-date= |url-status=}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale, the same as the animated film, while those surveyed by [[PostTrak]] gave it a 90% overall positive score, with 81% saying they would definitely recommend the film.<ref name=":2" />

Several changes made to the remake were criticized, including the removal of Pleakley's [[Drag (entertainment)|drag]] disguise, Gantu's absence, the characterization of Jumba, and the decision to have Nani surrender her guardianship of Lilo.<ref>{{cite news |last=Squires |first=Bethy |title=Disney Forgot What Ohana Means |url=https://www.vulture.com/article/lilo-and-stitch-live-action-ending.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250526010748/https://www.vulture.com/article/lilo-and-stitch-live-action-ending.html |archive-date=May 26, 2025 |access-date=May 26, 2025 |work=Vulture |date=May 25, 2025 |language=en|url-status=live}}</ref>

Brandon Yu of ''[[The New York Times]]'' gave the film a positive review and said, "It's a moderately fun, mostly serviceable and often adorable revamp."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Yu|first=Brandon|date=May 26, 2025|title=Lilo & Stitch Review: Creature Chaos|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/05/20/movies/lilo-and-stitch-review.html|website=[[The New York Times]]|access-date=January 8, 2026}}</ref> [[Nell Minow]] of ''[[RogerEbert.com]]'' gave the film three stars out of four and said, "Children new to the story will enjoy some gross-out humor, slapstick naughtiness, and the reassuring theme that families of all kinds, including those we choose, can be devoted to the idea of ohana."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Minow|first=Nell|date=May 22, 2025|title=Lilo & Stitch|url=https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/lilo-and-stitch-disney-remake-film-review-2025#google_vignette|website=[[RogerEbert.com]]|access-date=January 8, 2026}}</ref>

Helen O'Hara of ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]'' gave the film three stars out of five and said, "Fleischer Camp brings a light touch and a good human cast to this reverently faithful effort, but it's never as clear and bright as its source material."<ref>{{Cite web|last=O'Hara|first=Helen|date=May 20, 2025|title=Lilo & Stitch (2025) Review|url=https://www.empireonline.com/movies/reviews/lilo-and-stitch-2025/|website=[[Empire Magazine]]|access-date=January 10, 2026}}</ref> Eric Goldman of ''[[IGN]]'' gave the film a score of 8 out of 10 and said, "Ohana means family, and family means one of the better Disney live-action remakes."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Goldman|first=Eric|date=May 20, 2025|title=Lilo & Stitch Review|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/lilo-stitch-remake-review|website=[[IGN]]|access-date=January 8, 2026}}</ref>

Jesse Hassenger of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave the film one star out of five and saying, "director Dean Fleischer Camp (''Marcel the Shell with Shoes'') doesn't give either the poor humans or special-effects characters any kind of visible technical support in their impossible task."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Hassenger|first=Jesse|date=May 20, 2025|title=Lilo & Stitch|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2025/may/20/lilo-stitch-review-disney|website=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=January 8, 2026}}</ref> [[Bilge Ebiri]] of the ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]'' gave the film a negative review, saying, "It reminds us that ''Lilo & Stitch''{{'s}} form and its essence were one and the same. This remake doesn't feel like its own movie, but rather a doomed attempt to reengineer a miracle."<ref>{{Cite web|last=Ebiri|first=Bilge|date=May 21, 2025|title=Maybe a Live-Action Lilo & Stitch Remake Wasn't Such a Great Idea|url=https://www.vulture.com/article/review-disneys-lilo-and-stitch-remake-is-a-soulless-copy.html|website=[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]|access-date=January 10, 2026}}</ref>

==Sequel==
In an interview ahead of the film's release, director Fleischer Camp and producer Eirich said they were focused on the release of the current film and had not discussed concrete plans for a [[sequel]].
